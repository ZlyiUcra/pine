SB1 — squeeze breakout, how to read it

One signal. It predicts a single candle: the one that opens right after a decisive breakout out of a compressed range. Everything on the chart is either that signal or a measurement of how the signal has performed so far on this exact symbol and timeframe.

1. The entry marker — the only thing you need to act on

Large diamond BELOW the candle

Go long at the open of the next candle.

Large diamond ABOVE the candle

Go short at the open of the next candle.

Position carries the direction: below means buy, above means sell. Nothing else needs to be decoded. The marker sits on the candle after which you enter, appears at that candle's close, and cannot be turned off — there is no option for it.

The trigger candle closes outside a tight range of the previous 6 bars, with a body at least 1.5x the average body, closing in the outer 30% of its own range, on the right side of the EMA50, on above-average tick volume, and larger than the spread guard in pips.

The entry candle is the very next one. It is the candle being predicted, and the one you hold.

None of this can repaint. Every input comes from candles that are already closed, so once the large diamond appears it never moves and never disappears.

What is actually being scored is two things about the entry candle:

  1. Does it close in the signalled direction?
  2. Is its body larger than the average body of the previous 20 candles?

Nothing past that candle is measured.

3. The two small diamonds — information, not instruction

On the entry candle, one bar to the right of the large marker, a pair of small diamonds is painted. They are not extra signals. They are the same signal shown twice more, each copy coloured by a different running streak. Ignore them entirely if you only want to trade the large diamond.

Below the entry candle on a buy

Coloured by the current losing streak — how many signals in a row have failed right now (consecutiveCombinedFalse). On a sell the same copy sits above the candle and starts from red instead of teal.

Above the entry candle on a buy

Coloured by the current winning streak (consecutiveCombinedSuccess). On a sell this copy sits below the candle.

These two copies place themselves on both sides regardless of direction, so their position tells you nothing. That is fine now: direction is read off the large diamond one candle to the left. This pair was kept deliberately as an extra information layer.

4. Colour scale of the small pair

Both small diamonds use the same scale. It measures the streak against Min entries to start (default 10). Read it as "how far past the threshold are we".

teal — buy, loss streak below 9
red — sell, loss streak below 9
blue — win streak below 9
white — streak = 9 (one short)
lime — streak = 10 (threshold hit)
orange — streak = 11
violet — streak = 12
purple — streak 13 to 24
cyan — streak 25 and beyond

Deviation from C2. In C2 the colour functions had no else branch, so a streak below the threshold returned na and the diamond was invisible — C2 only ever showed signals that arrived deep into a streak. SB1 adds the else, so every signal is visible. To get C2 behaviour back, delete the two else lines at the end of getSignalColor and getOppositeSignalColor. The large entry diamond is unaffected either way.

5. How to trade it

  • Wait for a large diamond to appear at the close of a candle.
  • Direction: diamond below the candle = long, above = short.
  • Enter at the open of the very next candle.
  • Exit at the close of that same candle. Holding period is exactly one bar.
  • No stop, no target in what is measured. Adding them changes the outcome, and the table stops describing what you are doing.

The streak colours are a separate, optional bet. The C2 idea behind them: wait until the losing streak reaches a length the historical distribution says is near its maximum, then take the next signal on the theory that the run is due to end. The two side tables (Fails After Success, Successes After Fail) exist to test whether that holds on your data. Until the highlighted row in those tables sits well away from the small values, treat the streak colours as decoration rather than a rule.

6. The table in the bottom left, row by row

Three columns: Buy, Sell, Combined. Everything is measured on the bars visible to the script on this chart, this symbol, this timeframe. Change any of those and every number changes.

Bars analysed
Sample size. Small sample means every percentage below is noise.
Signals
How many times the signal fired.
Signals per 1000 bars
How rare it is. Below roughly 2 you do not have enough signals for the percentages to mean anything — you want at least 100 to 150 signals in total.
Direction correct
Raw count of signals where the entry candle closed the right way.
Direction accuracy
The same as a percentage. A coin flip gives 50%. From 52% to 56% is a real but small edge. Above 65% on a large sample, look for a bug before celebrating.
Dir + body > avg
Count of signals where both held: right direction and body larger than the 20-candle average. This is the event you originally asked about.
Joint accuracy
That count as a percentage of signals.
Random baseline
The same joint event measured over every bar on the chart, not just signal bars. What you would score firing at random. Typically 15% to 20%, because candle bodies are skewed — only about a third of candles exceed their own trailing average.
Edge over chance
Joint accuracy divided by the random baseline. The only row that matters. 1.00x means the signal contributes nothing. Green above 1.00x, brown below.
False Accuracy
Share of signals that got the direction wrong. Just 100% minus direction accuracy, kept because C2 had it.
Max False Streak
Longest run of consecutive wrong signals ever observed. Read it as the drawdown you must be able to sit through.
Max Success Streak
Longest run of consecutive correct signals.
Last 15 Signals
Five columns: #, Date / time (UTC+1), Type, Dir (1 if direction was right), Body (Big or Small). A row reading BUY / 1 / Big is a full hit on both conditions.
Why UTC+1
The timestamp is the open of the entry candle, printed in UTC+1 so it matches the Start / End indicator's hours filter, which uses the same offset. It is not the chart's timezone.

7. What this cannot do

The target you started from — 80% on both direction and body size for the next candle — is about 4.3x the random baseline. Edges that size do not exist on liquid FX at M1 to M5. Realistic outcomes on a decent sample are 1.1x to 1.5x, and the honest job of the table is to tell you when even that is absent.

Two things move the numbers more than any parameter tweak: the session filter (Asian hours on M1 are close to a random walk) and the spread guard in pips (on M1 EURUSD the average body is only a pip or two, so an unfiltered "large body" is mostly noise).
